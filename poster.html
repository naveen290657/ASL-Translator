📌 Abstract & Motivation

Over 70 million individuals worldwide rely on sign language as their primary mode of communication. Standard deep-learning vision models (CNNs) require bulky GPUs, exhibit high latency, and struggle with background noise and skin tone variations.

Machine Spectra 1.0 introduces a lightweight, edge-optimized assistive pipeline that converts live webcam feeds of ASL gestures into translated text and synthesized vocal speech in real-time, operating entirely on standard commodity CPUs.

⚙️ Technical Pipeline

1

Video Ingestion (OpenCV)

Captures live 60 FPS video feed with horizontal mirroring.

2

Landmark Extraction (MediaPipe)

Detects 21 3D hand joints (42 spatial coordinate values).

3

Spatial Normalization

Shifts coordinates to origin for scale and position invariance.

4

Random Forest Classification

Ensemble of 100 decision trees outputs prediction & confidence.

5

Debouncing & Speech (TTS)

10-frame confirmation buffer triggers spoken audio.

📐 Landmark Normalization Formula

To ensure the system works invariant to the user's distance from the camera or hand position on screen, coordinates are mathematically zero-centered:

x'ᵢ = xᵢ - min(X)   |   y'ᵢ = yᵢ - min(Y)
Yields a standardized 42-dimensional vector invariant to translation

• 21 Landmarks × 2 coordinates = 42 input features.
• Ignores background clutter, skin tone, clothing, and camera angles.

🔮 Future Scope & Conclusion

Conclusion: Machine Spectra demonstrates that geometric landmark extraction combined with ensemble learning offers a high-precision, zero-latency alternative to deep networks for edge sign translation.

Future Scope:
• Integrating Temporal LSTMs for dynamic continuous signs (e.g., 'J', 'Z').
• Two-handed interaction for full conversational ASL.
• Large Language Model (LLM) grammar synthesis.
